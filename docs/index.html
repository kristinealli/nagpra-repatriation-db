More Than Remains.

The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA) establishes a framework for the return of Native American ancestors and cultural items held by museums and federal agencies to lineal descendants, Tribes, and Native Hawaiian organizations. Repatriation is an important part of restoring cultural connections, respecting Tribal sovereignty, and addressing institutional responsibilities.

This project began with research into NAGPRA and the complex relationships among cultural items, institutions, Tribal communities, and repatriation processes. That research informed the design of a relational database and supporting applications intended to explore how technology can help organize and manage this information while recognizing that meaningful consultation, cultural authority, and repatriation decisions must remain with the communities involved.

Why this project

A path toward return..

Across the United States, Native American ancestors and cultural belongings remain in museums, universities, and federal collections. The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA) establishes responsibilities for institutions and processes for returning ancestors and certain cultural items to their communities. But repatriation involves more than identifying what an institution holds. It requires understanding relationships among ancestors, cultural belongings, Tribal communities, places, and institutional records. Those relationships are not always straightforward, and the information needed to support their return may be spread across multiple sources. This project began with the question: How can technology help organize those connections while respecting the cultural significance of the work and the authority of the communities involved?

Behind every record is a relationship. Behind every return is a community.

The goal was to explore how better information systems could support the work of bringing ancestors and cultural belongings home. I began by researching NAGPRA, the repatriation process, and the information relationships involved. That research informed a relational database designed to connect cultural items and ancestors with communities, institutions, locations, affiliations, and repatriation activities.

The resulting database and application prototype explore how records can be organized, connected, and reviewed.

My aim was to build technology designed in a manner that supports the process and the ones that carry out the work, not replace consultation, undermind Tribal authority, or replace community decision-making.

01 · Connect

Make Relationships Visible

The data base connects records about ancestors, cultural belongings, communities, institutions, and places to help reveal relationships that might otherwise remain fragmented across separate sources.

This was achieved via junction tables representing many-to-many relationships between items and discovery sites, linked remains profiles, institutional holdings, and affiliation records to preserve important context.

02 · Support

Represent the process of return

The workflow is intended to represent the consultation, affiliation, custody, requests, and transfers involved in repatriation by preserving the context needed to understand how records and responsibilities relate over time.

This was achieved by modeling the repatriation workflow through Consultation, RepatriationRequest, and Transfer records, alongside historical holdings and cultural affiliation determinations. These relationships were structured to track institutional custody, document consultation and requests, and represent the progression toward return.

03 · Respect

Sensitivity is a design requirement

It is important to recognize that despite being in institutional holding, information about ancestors and cultural belongings is not ordinary inventory data.

Sensitivity was incorporated into the project via role-based database access.

The prototype uses synthetic demonstration records and limited public-facing information, keeping sensitive data considerations and Tribal authority central to the design.

Workflow context

Tracking the Path Home

The National Park Service describes the following eight steps for museums and federal agencies handling Native American human remains and associated funerary objects. This summary is for context; refer to the current regulations and NPS guidance for the complete requirements.

01

Compile itemized list

Identify human remains and associated funerary objects in relevant holdings or collections.

02

Initiate consultation

Identify consulting parties and invite them to consult as soon as possible after compiling the list.

03

Consult on identifications

Discuss identifications with consulting parties and document concurrence, disagreement, or nonresponse.

04

Complete inventory

Submit the inventory, with clear determinations, to consulting parties and the National NAGPRA Program by the applicable deadline.

05

Submit completion notice

Submit a notice of inventory completion for publication in the Federal Register no later than six months after completing the inventory.

06

Consider requests

After publication, receive and consider requests from eligible lineal descendants, Tribes, or Native Hawaiian organizations.

07

Respond to requests

Within 90 days of receiving a request, send a written response to the requestor and other identified parties.

08

Complete repatriation

Within 90 days after responding, issue a written repatriation statement, as described in NPS guidance.

Deadlines and requirements depend on the applicable regulations and circumstances. This overview is not legal guidance and does not replace consultation or community decisions. See the National Park Service human-remains process and 43 CFR Part 10.

Database views that organize related records

These views support information review; they do not complete process steps, determine eligibility, or establish affiliation.

vw_hr_inventory_scope

Human-remains records with current holding and selected institutional, legal-status, and storage fields.

vw_hr_hazards

Items with known or noted potential hazards for handling review.

vw_hr_open_consultations

Open human-remains consultations, including status, meeting dates, and involved parties.

vw_hr_affiliations

Recorded human-remains affiliation determinations, including type, basis, date, and community.

vw_hr_unaffiliated_backlog

Human-remains records without a recorded affiliation, for follow-up review.

vw_pending_requests

Pending requests, requesting community, current holding institution, and decision status.

vw_pending_transfers

Approved requests with no completed transfer recorded and available scheduling information.

vw_completed_transfers

Completed transfer records, including parties, dates, and selected logistics.

Workflow views can contain operational information. The public demo uses only the separate, limited portfolio_item projection.

What the database tracks

A relational model built around people, provenance, custody, and process.

The coursework model organizes related records in four areas. It represents information for review; it does not make affiliation determinations or carry out repatriation.

01 · People & organizations

People & organizations

Community records represent Tribes and Native Hawaiian organizations; Institution records represent museums, universities, or agencies. Contact records link people and roles to a community or institution.

02 · Catalog & provenance

Catalog & provenance

CulturalItem represents categories such as human remains, funerary objects, sacred objects, and objects of cultural patrimony. RemainsProfile holds related details; DiscoverySite and ItemSite link items to findspots or reported and probable origins, including many-to-many relationships.

03 · Custody & affiliation

Custody & affiliation

Holding records track possession, control, or custody over time, along with acquisition and selected title or right-of-possession fields. Affiliation records store a lineal or cultural determination, its basis, date, and community; the software does not make that determination.

04 · Repatriation workflow

Repatriation workflow

Consultation records connect a community, institution, and item. RepatriationRequest records a community's formal request; Transfer records selected details when an approved return is carried out.

Project evolution

One idea, developed across multiple courses.

The project grew incrementally as I combined assignments from multiple courses into one sustained project focused on a shared goal.

Phase 1 · Database Management

MySQL relational database

Designed the schema, data dictionary, constraints, sample records, database privileges, and workflow-oriented views around the NAGPRA process.

Phase 2 · Programming

Python / Flet desktop application

Built a lightweight interface that loads an exported database view, browses and searches multiple fields, updates community information, and generates summary totals by community and category.

Phase 3 · Subsequent development

Supabase + GitHub Pages

Extended the database implementation with PostgreSQL/Supabase migrations and Row Level Security, then created a read-only web demonstration using a limited projection of synthetic data.

Responsible data design

Technical controls do not define appropriate data governance.

The underlying model includes fields for information that may require additional care, such as site coordinates, sensitive notes, storage information, and transfer details. The public demonstration uses synthetic data and omits those sensitive operational fields.

A limited public projection and access controls can reduce exposure, but technical safeguards alone do not establish culturally appropriate data governance. This educational project does not replace consultation, cultural knowledge, community authority or decision-making, or institutional responsibilities, and it is not an operational repatriation system.
