Portfolio project · database + application engineering

Modeling the information behind a complex repatriation workflow.

The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA) creates a process for returning Native American human remains and cultural items to lineal descendants, Tribes, and Native Hawaiian organizations. This project explores how a relational information system can connect the records involved in inventory, affiliation, consultation, formal requests, and transfer.

Why this project

Repatriation depends on connected information.

A repatriation case is not a single record. The process can involve an item or ancestor, the institution holding it, one or more communities, provenance and site information, affiliation evidence, consultations, requests, legal status, and transfer history.

I built this project to explore how database design can make those relationships easier to organize and follow without reducing a culturally significant process to a simple inventory list.

01 · Connect

Bring related records together

The schema links communities, institutions, contacts, cultural items, remains profiles, discovery sites, holdings, affiliations, consultations, repatriation requests, and transfers.

02 · Follow

Represent the workflow, not just the objects

SQL views surface practical checkpoints such as human-remains inventory, open consultations, affiliation backlog, pending requests, pending transfers, and completed transfers.

03 · Protect

Treat sensitivity as a design requirement

The data model includes restricted-site flags and sensitive-note fields. The public portfolio layer exposes only a deliberately limited projection rather than the operational schema.

Workflow context

From inventory to return.

The database was organized around the major stages described in the NAGPRA repatriation process. The goal was to make related records and status checkpoints easier to retrieve as a case moves forward.

01

Inventory

Identify and document human remains and cultural items held by an institution.

02

Consultation

Identify relevant parties, begin consultation, and record ongoing communication and status.

03

Identification

Connect items to provenance, site information, and potential or determined affiliations.

04

Inventory completion

Track inventory milestones and the records needed for required notices and review.

05

Request

Record formal repatriation requests, requesting communities, dates, and decisions.

06

Transfer

Track approved returns through scheduling, completion, receiving parties, and transfer history.

This visualization is a simplified project-oriented view of the workflow, not legal guidance.

What the database tracks

A relational model built around people, provenance, custody, and process.

P

People & organizations

Community, Institution, and Contact records connect Tribes/NHOs, museums or agencies, and the people participating in the work.

C

Catalog & provenance

CulturalItem, RemainsProfile, DiscoverySite, and ItemSite represent what is being tracked and where it may have originated.

H

Custody & affiliation

Holding records capture possession/control over time, while Affiliation connects an item to a lineal descendant or community determination.

R

Repatriation workflow

Consultation, RepatriationRequest, and Transfer represent the case workflow from engagement through return.

Project evolution

One idea, developed across database and programming coursework.

The project grew in stages rather than being rebuilt from scratch for a portfolio.

Phase 1 · Database Management

MySQL relational database

Designed the schema, data dictionary, constraints, sample records, database privileges, and workflow-oriented views around the NAGPRA process.

Phase 2 · Programming

Python / Flet desktop application

Built a lightweight interface that loads an exported database view, browses and searches multiple fields, updates community information, and generates summary totals by community and category.

Phase 3 · Portfolio modernization

Supabase + GitHub Pages

Ported the database to PostgreSQL/Supabase, added production migrations and Row Level Security, and created a read-only public web experience using a sanitized portfolio projection.

Responsible data design

Not every field belongs on a public website.

The underlying schema anticipates information that may require additional care, including site coordinates, site-sensitive notes, consultation notes, storage information, chain-of-custody records, and other operational details.

For the portfolio version, the browser receives only the fields needed to demonstrate search, filtering, relationships, and application architecture. This separation is intentional: the technical goal is to demonstrate the system without treating access to sensitive information as a feature.
