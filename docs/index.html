More Than Remains.

The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA) establishes a framework for the return of Native American ancestors and cultural items held by museums and federal agencies to lineal descendants, Tribes,and Native Hawaiian organizations. Repatriation is an important part of restoring cultural connections, respecting Tribal sovereignty, and addressing institutional responsibilities.

This project began with research into NAGPRA and the complex relationships among cultural items, institutions, Tribal communities, and repatriation processes. That research informed the design of a relational database and supporting applications intended to explore how technology can help organize and manage this information while recognizing that meaningful consultation, cultural authority, and repatriation decisions must remain with the communities involved.

Why this project

Repatriation depends on connected information.

NAGPRA includes a process of dialogue between covered institutions and Native communities. Repatriation records can connect ancestors and cultural items, institutional holdings, communities, provenance, affiliations, consultations, requests, and transfers. Organizing these relationships was the starting point for this project.

I began by researching NAGPRA and its information relationships, then used database and application design to explore how those records could be organized without reducing a culturally significant process to an inventory list.

02 · Follow

Represent the workflow, not just the objects

03 · Protect

Treat sensitivity as a design requirement

The model accounts for information that may need additional care. The public demonstration uses synthetic data and exposes only a deliberately limited projection, rather than the full database model.

Workflow context

Tracking the Path Home

The database was organized around selected information and workflow concepts associated with NAGPRA. It is a simplified educational model, not a complete interpretation of NAGPRA. Its goal is to make related records and status checkpoints easier to organize, not to replace consultation or community decisions.

01

Inventory

Identify and document human remains and cultural items held by an institution.

02

Identify relevant parties, begin consultation, and record ongoing communication and status.

03

04

Track inventory milestones and the records needed for required notices and review.

05

Record formal repatriation requests, requesting communities, dates, and decisions.

06

Transfer

Track approved returns through scheduling, completion, receiving parties, and transfer history.

This is a simplified educational visualization, not a complete NAGPRA or CalNAGPRA workflow, legal guidance, or a substitute for consultation.

What the database tracks

A relational model built around people, provenance, custody, and process.

P

People & organizations

Community, Institution, and Contact records connect Tribes/NHOs, museums or agencies, and the people participating in the work.

C

Catalog & provenance

CulturalItem, RemainsProfile, DiscoverySite, and ItemSite represent what is being tracked and where it may have originated.

H

Custody & affiliation

Holding records capture possession/control over time, while Affiliation connects an item to a lineal descendant or community determination.

R

Repatriation workflow

Consultation, RepatriationRequest, and Transfer represent the case workflow from engagement through return.

Project evolution

One idea, developed across multiple courses.

The project grew incrementally as I combined assignments from multiple courses into one sustained project focused on a shared goal.

Phase 1 · Database Management

MySQL relational database

Designed the schema, data dictionary, constraints, sample records, database privileges, and workflow-oriented views around the NAGPRA process.

Phase 2 · Programming

Python / Flet desktop application

Built a lightweight interface that loads an exported database view, browses and searches multiple fields, updates community information, and generates summary totals by community and category.

Phase 3 · Subsequent development

Supabase + GitHub Pages

Extended the database implementation with PostgreSQL/Supabase migrations and Row Level Security, then created a read-only web demonstration using a limited projection of synthetic data.

Responsible data design

Technical controls do not define appropriate data governance.

The underlying model includes fields for information that may require additional care, such as site coordinates, sensitive notes, storage information, and transfer details. The public demonstration uses synthetic data and omits those sensitive operational fields.

A limited public projection and access controls can reduce exposure, but technical safeguards alone do not establish culturally appropriate data governance. This educational project does not replace consultation, cultural knowledge, community authority or decision-making, or institutional responsibilities, and it is not an operational repatriation system.

About this portfolio project

This project was originally developed at Bay Mills Community College as coursework in Database Management and then incorporated into a Programming Project. It combines relational database design, Python application development, SQL reporting, web development, cloud-hosted PostgreSQL, and access-control concepts in one evolving project.
